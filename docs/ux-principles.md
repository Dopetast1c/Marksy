# Marksy UX: context, principles, decisions and what is open for review

Read this before changing any screen, string or component. `docs/design.md` says what each screen contains; `docs/mockups.html` shows it; this document says **why** it is that way, which parts are settled and which parts are open. A UX review that ignores it will undo decisions that were made for a reason and get reverted.

## 1. Who uses this and when

- **A parent**, two or three times a week, usually in the evening, on a phone, often one-handed, for under a minute. Wants: is anything new, is anything bad, what is tomorrow, how is each subject going. Several children, so every question is "for which child?" first.
- **A child (grade 5 and up)**, after school, on a phone. Wants: what did I get today, my average in a subject, what do I need to reach a target, what is for tomorrow, what must I not forget. Should feel like the app is on their side, not a surveillance tool.
- **A younger child (grades 1 to 4)**: no marks in Belarus for grades 1 and 2; schedule, homework and "don't forget" matter. Simple mode exists for them.
- **The weekend planner**: a parent on Saturday morning deciding how much free time the week allows. Needs the next week's tests, poems to learn, things to bring, in one place.
- **A second parent, grandparents, a friend's family**: same app, own login, own notification settings, possibly another diary system or another country.

The source data is a school diary (Belarus `diary.e-schools.by` first). It is read-only, slow to query and refreshed a few times a day, so the app never promises real time; it always shows when data was last updated.

## 2. Principles

1. **Overview first, detail one tap away.** Главная is only what matters now. Lists and tables live in their own tabs.
2. **Numbers come with what they were.** Every average and count shows the previous calendar period's value next to it, with a small direction arrow: "7.3 ↓ · было 8.1"; what "было" is compared with is written once, small, in the period navigator ("12–18 окт · против 5–11 окт"), not under every number. The size of the change is not printed; the previous value is what parents asked for, and the difference is one subtraction. Two colour systems never mix on one element: the value's colour means its band, the arrow's colour means direction, and change is never drawn as a filled badge (fills are for mark chips only).
3. **Calendar, not rolling windows.** "Неделя" starts on the first day of the school week, "Месяц" on the 1st, "Четверть" on the term start. Parents and schools think in calendar units.
4. **One kind of information, one component.** Average with distribution is StatBlock everywhere (Главная, Оценки, Предмет). A mark is MarkChip. A subject line is SubjectRow. Variants are props, never copies.
5. **Colour never carries meaning alone.** Every band colour comes with the number or a label. The mark value is always visible on the chip.
6. **Facts and advice are separable.** Guidance (goals, the hints in "Обратить внимание", lesson hints) can be switched off per family and per user; what remains is complete.
7. **Rules, not constants.** The provider says what exists, the grading scale says how marks look and compare, the family's rules say what matters (alert thresholds, what counts as low, prep days, the family's tags). Defaults come from the scale; everything is editable.
8. **Nothing is Belarusian in the UI code.** Scale, capabilities, calendar, names, languages come from the provider manifest or the user. The Belarusian interface is the all-capabilities-on instance and must not change when the model grows.
9. **Every subject name is a link** to the subject screen, with period and student preserved, and Back returns exactly where you were.
10. **Plain language.** No system vocabulary ("webhook", "sync run" only on the Sync screen), no filler, no exclamation marks, short sentences. Russian copy spells the language «беларуский». UI strings are keys; twelve languages.
11. **Thumb reach and one row.** Primary controls at the bottom (tab bar) or in a single header row; headers never cost more than one row plus the controls that drive the screen.
12. **Show the effect before saving.** Any rule editor has a live preview on real data ("Проверка на расписании", "Проверка на данных").

Neutral copy, no causation claims | Parents want to know how long the child really works; a child who forgets the timer should still leave a readable trace, and a child who ticks everything at once should be visible as that fact rather than as fake minutes | per-homework stopwatch; counting ticks as time without marking them as estimates; hiding the data from the child; "больше занимался → выше оценка" copy; a correlation coefficient | settled
| D22 | Data freshness is shown only when it is a problem: nothing when fresh (footer line on Главная), one `FreshnessBanner` under the header on every student screen when data is older than a day, syncing is paused or the password failed; it opens the Sync screen, which explains what happened and what the next run will take | The server is a home machine that can be off for days and the diary can be down; a parent must never mistake stale data for "no new marks", but a permanent status line would be noise | always-visible "last updated" in the header; silent staleness; toast on every failed run | settled |
| D49 | The no-marks mode of Главная, Предметы and Предмет (lessons and homework instead of averages, bands and goals) switches on from the data alone: the student's school year has no grading scale (`student_years.grading_scale_id = null`, sent by the provider as `Calendar.gradingScaleId = null`). The provider knows when its system gives no marks; the interface never looks at a grade number | The age at which marks start differs per country and school (Belarus: from grade 3; elsewhere grade 1, 5 or never); a grade check in the UI would be a Belarusian rule in the country-agnostic code and would break for the second provider | `level <= 2` in components (the prototype's shortcut); a per-family switch "отметки не ставят" | settled |

## 4. Invariants (do not change in a UX review)

- Tab structure (D1), period order and default (D2), calendar alignment (principle 3).
- Band roles and the Belarusian ranges (D3); mark value always visible on a chip (principle 5).
- StatBlock content and its presence on Главная and Предмет (not on Оценки, which is the list: D30), with deltas (D5), and the missing-data states (D23): no arrow without two real values, "—" for no marks.
- Finals live on their own screen with an entry row on Оценки and Предметы (D24); the period navigator is the first body line on every period screen (D2).
- The two-section subject screen and what the filters govern (D6).
- Student picker rule and the avatar-only switcher (D7, D8, D15).
- Rules-not-constants layering (principle 7) and provider neutrality (principle 8): no number about marks, no term word, no weekday assumption in a component.
- One component per kind of information (principle 4): if a review wants a different look for StatBlock, change StatBlock once.
- Every subject name is a link (principle 9).
- Fictional data in the repository (D16).
- Guidance remains switchable and the screens stay complete without it (principle 6).
- Stale or paused data is announced on every student screen, in one banner, and never silently (D22). The wording may change; the presence may not.

## 5. Open for review (change freely, keep the reasons above)



- Visual polish within the tokens: spacing, type scale, radii, shadows, dark theme contrast, icon set, the avatar drawing style.
- Copy: every string, label and empty state (keep plain language; keep «беларуский»).
- Order and grouping **inside** sections: cards within "За период", rows within Ещё, fields within the rule editors.
- Empty, loading and error states; first-run onboarding after connecting a diary; the install prompt.
- The editors (tags, alert rules): flow, grouping, defaults shown, how the preview reads.
- Family, Sync, Notifications and avatar screens: layout and hierarchy.
- Tablet and desktop layouts (two columns, persistent navigation) and landscape phone.
- Accessibility: focus order, touch targets (44 px), reduced motion, contrast in both themes, screen-reader labels.
- Micro-interactions: switching a child, marking a lesson ready, saving a tag or a rule.
- Simple mode visuals (D20) and the student-mode variant of each screen.
- The week view of the schedule and its "Обратить внимание" card.

Anything in section 4 that a reviewer believes is wrong goes into a proposal with the reason, not into a change.

## 6. How to make a change

1. Change `docs/mockups.html` (it is the visual reference and runs without a build) and the matching lines in `docs/design.md` in the same commit. Add a row to the decision log above when a reason changes.
2. Keep the prototype running: the script must parse (`node --check` on the extracted script) and every screen must render on every scale and every capability combination (the rail toggles). The Belarusian all-on state must look the same unless the change is about it.
3. Screenshot before and after at 390 px width; put both in the pull request.
4. No new literal mark numbers, term words or weekday indices in components; no second implementation of an existing component.
5. If a change needs new data from the diary, it is a provider capability first (`docs/providers.md`), a UI change second.

## 7. Brief for a UX review agent (copy as the task)

> You are reviewing the UX of Marksy, a family app for school marks. Read, in this order: `docs/ux-principles.md` (context, principles, decision log, invariants, open areas), `docs/design.md` sections 4 and 5 (screens), and open `docs/mockups.html` in a browser (use the rail to switch role, student, scale, calendar and provider capabilities, and the screens list to reach every screen).

Write copy in Russian in the prototype and in English in the docs; no em-dashes.
